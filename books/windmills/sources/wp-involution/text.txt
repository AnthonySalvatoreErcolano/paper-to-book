Euclidean geometry
[

]
A simple example of an involution of the three-dimensional

Another involution is

Projective geometry
[
edit
]
An involution is a

of period 2, that is, a projectivity that interchanges pairs of points.
[
6
]
:
24
Any projectivity that interchanges two points is an involution.
The three pairs of opposite sides of a

meet any line (not through a vertex) in three pairs of an involution. More generally, given four points and any line not through one of them, there exists a projective involution which exchanges any point on this line with the second intersection of the (possibly degenerate) conic through the given point and the four others. This result has been called

[

Its origins can be seen in Lemma IV of the lemmas to the

If an involution has one

The operation of complement in

In the study of
