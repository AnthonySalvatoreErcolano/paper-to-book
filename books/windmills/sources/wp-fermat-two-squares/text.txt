The statement that every prime

Proof with partition theory
[
edit
]
In 2016, A. David Christopher gave a

)

[

]
The proof has a similar structure to Zagier's proof in its use of involutions but the sets and involutions involved are a bit easier to visualize. In fact we can take the word 'squares' in the theorem literally and attack the problem of partitioning a prime into two squares by studying the more general problem of partitioning it into two rectangles.
Consider a set of
p

On the set

−

of the two rectangles would be even and so this case does not occur. It follows that each partition of

as the sum of two squares corresponds to a fixed point under this involution and we will show that these fixed points exist by showing that the number of elements of
𝒱
is odd, which, as in Zagier's proof implies that the number of fixed points is odd as well and hence non-zero.
Showing that

1. Showing that

|

∖

|

The second step is the easiest of the two. Each good-but-not-very-good partition of

|

𝒱

Now to show that the set

It is easy to visualize these fixed points as they all have the same symmetric shape: an
